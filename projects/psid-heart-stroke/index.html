Heart Attack & Stroke Survivors in the PSID

Building an analytic dataset from nine waves of the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (2005–2021) to compare the demographic and socioeconomic profile of adults with and without a history of heart attack or stroke.

Question

Cardiovascular disease was the leading cause of death in the United States in 2022, with 941,652 deaths (American Heart Association, 2025), and social and economic conditions shape who has a heart attack or stroke. The project set out to describe the adults who have survived a heart attack or stroke, compared with adults who have never had one.

What I did

Build the dataset

Merged family- and individual-level files from nine biennial PSID waves in SAS 9.4, linked people across waves, kept one record per person and harmonized variables using each wave’s codebook.

Check the cohort

Identified participants who had died by linking each wave’s death records to identifiers from earlier waves, and wrote a data dictionary for the analytic dataset.

Define groups and variables

Classified adults by history of heart attack or stroke, including 5- and 10-year survivors, and prepared 11 characteristics: age, sex, race and ethnicity, region, metropolitan area, marital status, number of children, health insurance, education, occupation and family income.

Estimate

Produced survey-weighted percentages with 95% confidence intervals using the PSID sampling weights, and tested differences between adults with and without a history of heart attack or stroke.

Skills

  • Working with a large longitudinal survey: merging files, linking people across waves and documenting every variable.
  • Survey-weighted estimation in SAS 9.4 with 95% confidence intervals.
  • Data quality checks against each wave’s codebook before analysis.
