Heart Attack & Stroke Survivors in the PSID

Who are the U.S. adults who have survived a heart attack or stroke? A descriptive profile of their age, income, education and living situation, compared with adults who have never had one, using nine waves of the Panel Study of Income Dynamics (2005–2021).

Question

Cardiovascular disease was the leading cause of death in the United States in 2022, with 941,652 deaths (American Heart Association, 2025). Social and economic conditions shape who has a heart attack or stroke and how people live afterwards. This project describes the demographic and socioeconomic profile of adults with a history of heart attack or stroke, compared with adults who have never had one, to help target prevention and support for survivors.

What I did

Build the dataset

Merged family- and individual-level files from nine biennial PSID waves in SAS 9.4, linked people across waves, kept one record per person and harmonized variables using each wave’s codebook.

Check the cohort

Identified participants who had died by linking each wave’s death records to identifiers from earlier waves, and wrote a data dictionary for the analytic dataset.

Estimate

Produced weighted percentages with 95% confidence intervals using the PSID sampling weights, and tested differences between adults with and without a history of heart attack or stroke.

Communicate

Wrote a draft manuscript and presented the findings to the branch at CDC’s Division for Heart Disease and Stroke Prevention.

Findings

  • Older. 72% of survivors were 65 or older, compared with 28% of adults with no history.
  • Lower income. 34% of survivors lived in families earning under $25,000 a year, compared with 18%; only 15% earned $100,000 or more, compared with 32%.
  • Less education, more often living without a spouse. 57% of survivors had no college education (vs. 40%) and 55% were not married (vs. 44%).
  • More often men and rural. 54% of survivors were men (vs. 47%) and 24% lived outside metropolitan areas (vs. 18%).
  • Five- and ten-year survivors had a similar profile; for example, 33% and 34% were in the lowest income band.

Limitations

  • This is a descriptive comparison: percentages are not adjusted for age. Survivors are much older, which explains part of the differences, such as marital status and health insurance through Medicare.
  • History of heart attack or stroke comes from survey reports, not medical records.
  • The results show who survivors are, not what causes heart attacks or strokes.
